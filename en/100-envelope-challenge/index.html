100 envelope challenge: how it works, plus a printable chart

Number 100 envelopes, draw one, and put in the amount on it. No envelopes? Tick the chart instead — it works the same.

Key facts

  • The 100 envelope challenge: number envelopes 1 to 100 and put $n in envelope n.
  • All 100 envelopes add up to $5,050 (1 + 2 + … + 100).
  • One envelope a day takes 100 days; two a week takes 50 weeks.
  • The biggest envelope is $100; the average is $50.50.
  • Scale it: $0.50 per number gives $2,525; $2 per number gives $10,100.

How the 100 envelope challenge works

  1. Number 100 envelopes, or use the chart. You do not need real envelopes — ticking the chart below works the same.
  2. Draw one. Shuffle and draw like a lottery (the original way), or pick one you can afford.
  3. Put in the number in dollars. Envelope 37 gets $37. Tick it on the chart.
  4. Fill all 100. You have $5,050.

Total = $1 × (1 + 2 + … + 100) = $1 × 100 × 101 ÷ 2 = $5,050

Tips to finish

  • One a day means up to $100 in a single day. Two a week (50 weeks) is a gentler pace.
  • Keeping cash at home? Move it to a bank account before it grows large.
  • Too big? Use $0.50 per number for a $2,525 version.

Questions

How much is the 100 envelope challenge?

$5,050 when envelope n holds $n. At $0.50 per number it is $2,525.

How long does it take?

100 days at one envelope a day, or 50 weeks at two a week. Any pace works.

Do I need 100 real envelopes?

No. Tick the chart instead — the total is the same.

Is there a version for other currencies?

Yes. The blank PDF has numbers 1–100 only, so you can pick your own unit.
